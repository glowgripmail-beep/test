# Ad Vision Writing

A vision is a play-by-play of a video that does not exist yet, narrated in the order the viewer will experience it, so that an editor can cut it and a copywriter can script it without asking a single question.

It is not a script. It is not a table. It is not a list of ideas. It is one continuous piece of spoken-sounding prose, roughly 150 to 350 words, that a person could read out loud while the imaginary video plays in their head.

Three failure modes to avoid before anything else:
- **Writing a script.** A vision describes and explains. It quotes exactly one line verbatim. Everything else is narrated.
- **Writing an essay about the strategy.** Strategy appears as a short footer, not as the body. The body is what happens on screen.
- **Writing something that could be about any product.** If you can swap the product name and the vision still works, it is worthless. Every vision must be impossible for a competitor to reuse.

Read `references/anatomy.md` before writing the first vision in a session. It contains four gold-standard visions broken down beat by beat with the reasoning behind every choice. Read `references/devices.md` when picking the persuasion machinery for a specific vision. Read `references/template.md` for the Hebrew skeleton, connector bank and a worked example.

## The reference comes first

A vision is always downstream of something that already worked. Before writing, establish what the reference is:

- an outside ad that was swiped (a viral format, a competitor ad, a sketch, a news format)
- an existing winner of ours whose hook or structure we are reusing
- footage we already shot that we are re-cutting

Open the vision by naming it. `סרטון של Zillow פורמט אחד`, `שאלוני רחוב, להשתמש בסרטון שעשינו עם אריאל ושון`, `ההוק מתחיל אותו דבר כמו הווינרים העיקריים שלנו`. This single line tells the editor the visual language, the pacing and the energy without describing any of them.

If a reference exists, first understand what the reference does beat by beat and why it holds attention, then write the vision as that skeleton filled with our product. If no reference exists, say so explicitly in the vision and name the closest format instead.

## The twelve components

Every vision carries these. They appear in this order because that is the order the viewer meets them.

1. **Reference anchor and format.** What it is modelled on, and the shooting format: first person, side shot, street interview, VSL over b-roll, faceless voiceover, news opener.
2. **Avatar and awareness level.** Who is watching and how much they already know (Schwartz: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware). This decides everything downstream.
3. **The hook, as a shot.** What the viewer sees in the first second and what emotion the video enters on. Never "an attention-grabbing opener." Always a describable image plus a named emotion.
4. **The one line, verbatim, in quotes.** The line immediately after the hook is the most important line in the ad. Write it exactly as it will be said. It is the only verbatim text in the document.
5. **Beats in order, glued causally.** Each beat connects to the next with `וכך`, `אז`, `ולכן`, `בגלל ש`, `ואז`. The glue is the argument. A beat that does not earn the next beat gets cut.
6. **The one objection this video exists to kill.** Name it. One video kills one objection.
7. **The unique feature and what it means.** Always placed late, after the rational ground has been cleared. The feature is the fact; the meaning is why the buyer wants it.
8. **Price and offer placement, including withholding.** State where the price lands and whether it is deliberately delayed. State the offer.
9. **Proof placement.** Where social proof enters, and in what form.
10. **CTA.** In our house style, soft and quiz-like.
11. **Strategy footer.** Awareness level and emotional arc in one or two sentences.
12. **Production notes.** What footage exists, what needs shooting, what gets blurred or blacked out, what to keep short.

If a vision is missing 4, 6 or 8, it is not finished. Those three are the ones people skip and they are the ones that decide whether the ad converts.

## Writing rules

**Present tense, narration order.** `מתחילים ב...`, `אז הוא...`, `ואז עוברים ל...`, `אחרי זה אנחנו מדברים על...`. Write as if you are watching it.

**Exactly one verbatim line.** Adding a second dilutes the first. The editor needs to know which sentence carries the ad.

**Numbers, always.** `1,000 שקל`, `20 דגמים בעולם`, `90 יום`, `1+1`, `316L`. Vague is unshootable and unpersuasive. Specificity is the persuasion.

**Name the enemy out loud.** Rolex, Patek, AP, [החלופה המוכרת], [המתחרה], [החלופה היקרה]. Naming what we are not is faster than describing what we are.

**Write at least one negative instruction.** `המראיין לא עונה על השאלה כמה זה עולה`, `ההתלבטות ממש קצרה`, `אין פוקוס על solution aware`. What the video refuses to do is as much a decision as what it does, and it is the part an editor will get wrong by default.

**Every beat answers one question:** why does the viewer keep watching after this. If you cannot answer it, the beat is decoration.

**One job per vision.** Status transformation, or expert validation, or category differentiation, or gift occasion. Not two.

**No tables, no bullet lists, no timestamps, no "this will be powerful."** Adjectives about how good the ad will be are noise. Music, fonts and grading appear only when they carry meaning.

## Batches

When asked for several visions, they must attack different rungs, not variations of one ad. A healthy batch of four covers:

- one unaware or problem-unaware entry, pure story or entertainment, no problem stated
- one problem aware, often a different avatar or a different buying occasion
- one solution aware, evaluation and comparison, price handled
- one product aware, why us and not the others in our category

Same product, four different doors. If two visions could be A/B variants of each other, one of them is wasted.

## House structure

For our brand, the beat order inside the body follows the mandatory ad structure: a hook that opens on the desire, then calm semi-scientific dissonance explaining why what she tried was never going to work, then courage (it is safe, the price is worth it, money back), then a soft quiz-style CTA. A vision maps the reference onto this skeleton. When the reference fights the skeleton, the skeleton wins and the vision says which beat of the reference was dropped and why.

Claims discipline applies inside visions too. A vision that describes a beat we are not allowed to say is a vision that will die in review. Support language, never cure language. If a reference's power came from a claim we cannot make, the vision must state what replaces it.

## Quality gates

Run these before handing a vision over. Any no means rewrite.

- Could an editor cut this without asking a single question?
- Is there exactly one sentence in quotation marks?
- Can you name in five words the single objection it kills?
- Does the price have a stated position, even if that position is "withheld until the end"?
- Does it tell the editor at least one thing not to do?
- Is the awareness level stated?
- Swap in a competitor's product. Does it break? It must break.
- Read it out loud. Does it sound like a person describing a video, or like a document?
