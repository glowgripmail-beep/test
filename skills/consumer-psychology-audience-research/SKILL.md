# Consumer Psychology and Audience Research

One skill for the "who buys and why" layer that sits under copy, ads and store design: the psychology of buying decisions, the method for researching customers and the market, and what is specific to Israeli consumers.

Merged from three source skills. Each source lives in its own folder as `GUIDE.md`, kept close to verbatim:

| Part | Folder | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| consumer-psychology | `consumer-psychology/GUIDE.md` | LF8 desires, the 9 learned wants, Cialdini as a diagnostic checklist, conversion biases, objection psychology, pricing psychology, how to log and diagnose creatives. |
| customer-market-research | `customer-market-research/GUIDE.md` | Research method: sources to mine in priority order, VOC categories, 7-step process, research document template, five depth questions per avatar, survey question design. |
| israeli-audience | `israeli-audience/GUIDE.md` | The Israeli consumer: freier anxiety, skepticism, dugri, "people like me" proof, Hebrew language rules, trust signals, checkout and payments, consumer-protection basics, the Israeli calendar, segments, platforms. |

## Conflicts between sources

Not resolved here. Each side is labeled by source. Several conflicts are with ecommerce-store-cro (part: store-design-cro), which states that for store and design questions it overrides consumer-psychology and israeli-audience because it has sources.

1. **Demographics in avatars.** customer-market-research: "Instead of building around age/gender/city, build around" the five depth questions. israeli-audience: slang calibrated to avatar age (18–25 vs 35+), segments (secular / traditional / religious / Haredi, center vs periphery, Russian- and Arabic-speaking) and platform split by age.
2. **Decoy effect.** consumer-psychology lists "Decoy/comparison" (cost-per-day vs. the expensive alternative) as a bias that moves e-com conversion. ecommerce-store-cro (store-design-cro, section 5): [שנוי במחלוקת] the researchers who found the decoy effect wrote in 2014 that it rarely occurs in the real market; do not build an offer on it.
3. **Charm pricing.** consumer-psychology: "charm pricing (X99/X90)". store-design-cro section 5: a 9-ending raised demand, but only when the left digit changes; 199 vs 200 matters, 189 vs 190 probably not.
4. **Installments.** israeli-audience: installments expected above ~₪150; display them. store-design-cro section 9: the share of online purchases made in installments is [לא אומת], and only one observed store advertised installments on the page.
5. **Checkout fields in Israel.** israeli-audience: "Every extra field in checkout = drop-off; Israelis abandon fast." store-design-cro section 9: field counts and abandonment reasons come from US shoppers; there is no Israeli data.
6. **Money-back guarantee.** israeli-audience lists it first among trust signals that convert in Israel. store-design-cro section 5: a money-back guarantee raises purchase intent but the effect is moderate, and a "double refund" adds nothing.
7. **Urgency.** consumer-psychology: "frame what they LOSE by waiting", "consequence of inaction + real deadline". store-design-cro section 5: [שנוי במחלוקת] timers: limited-time offers usually raise product evaluation, but at one hour or less spending dropped; aggressive pressure tactics caused abandonment and anger; regulators treat fake timers and stock claims as deception.
