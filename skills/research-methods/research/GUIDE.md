# Research

Based on STORM (Stanford OVAL). One question asked from one angle gives back one angle, and whatever that angle missed never shows up. This skill asks it from five angles, then verifies before it writes anything down.

## Step 1 - Frame the topic
Restate the research question in one sentence. List what a good answer has to contain. If the question is vague, ask ONE clarifying question, then keep going.

## Step 2 - Spin up five experts
Run five researchers in parallel, one per lens. Each one searches on its own and does not see the others' work:
1. The Practitioner - has actually done this. What breaks in the real world, what the day to day looks like, what nobody writes down.
2. The Academic - what the research and the primary sources actually say. Studies, papers, documented methods, sample sizes.
3. The Skeptic - actively tries to kill the idea. Failure cases, counter-evidence, who lost money, what the hype is hiding.
4. The Economist - the money. Costs, pricing, incentives, who profits, what it replaces, second-order effects.
5. The Historian - what came before. Prior attempts, what failed and why, what is already settled.

Each expert returns: findings, the source behind each finding, and a confidence level.

## Step 3 - Map the disagreements
Put the five sets side by side and write out explicitly:
- Where the experts AGREE. Converging evidence, treat as strong.
- Where they DISAGREE, and the exact fact that would settle it.
- What each expert found that no other expert surfaced.

Disagreement is the signal. Do not smooth it into an average.

## Step 4 - Verification pass
Before a single claim gets written into the final answer, fact-check every source:
- Open the source. Does it actually say the thing being claimed.
- Is it primary (study, docs, filing, first-hand) or someone repeating someone else.
- Is it current, and is there a newer version that changed the answer.
- Does the source sell something related to the claim.

Any claim that fails this pass gets cut or demoted, never softened.

## Step 5 - Rank by reliability
Tag every surviving finding:
- HIGH - primary source, multiple experts landed on it independently, no credible counter-evidence.
- MEDIUM - one solid source, or good sources that partly conflict.
- LOW - single weak source, secondhand, or from a party with an interest in the answer.

## Step 6 - Report
Return this, in this order, and keep it tight:
1. The answer in three sentences.
2. HIGH-confidence findings, source next to each.
3. MEDIUM and LOW findings, labeled.
4. Where the experts disagreed, and what would settle it.
5. BLIND SPOTS. The angles none of the five covered, and what to research next.

No brain dumps. If a finding does not change what the reader would do, cut it.
